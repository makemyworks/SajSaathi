1. The idea

Saj is getting ready — adornment, grooming, care. Saathi is a companion. The mark is an S drawn from two crescent strokes that meet and hand over to each other: a teal stroke (trust, calm, the provider) and a gold stroke (warmth, care, the customer). Two companions forming one letter.

The earlier artwork used a woman's face, a leaf and scissors. Saj Saathi serves men, women, families, older adults and pets, so the face and scissors were removed. The teal-and-gold ribbon S — the strongest idea in that artwork — is kept and simplified so it stays recognisable at 16 px, on an embroidered uniform or on a shop sign.

2. Logo versions

Primary: horizontal lockup. Stacked: square spaces (posters, packaging fronts). Symbol alone: app icons, favicons, avatars, embroidery, stamps. Wordmark alone: only where the symbol already appears nearby. The wordmark is Lora SemiBold converted to outlines — never retype it.

3. Clear space and minimum size

Clear space: keep empty space around the logo equal to the height of the symbol's upper (teal) crescent — about ½ of the symbol height — on all sides.

5. Typography

Lora (headings, wordmark) and Mukta (body, Hindi/Devanagari and Latin in one family, designed by Ek Type in India). Both are free under the SIL Open Font License and are bundled into the apps at build time. Mukta keeps Hindi and English text the same height and weight, so bilingual screens look like one design. For other Indian scripts use the matching Noto family until a reviewed translation and font pairing are chosen.

6. Backgrounds

On photographs, place the logo on a calm area or on a solid teal/ivory band. On single-colour print (stamps, receipts, laser-cut signs) use the monochrome version.

7. Don't

Also don't: bring back the face, scissors or leaf illustration; retype the name in another font; write it as “SajSaathi”, “Saj-Saathi” or “SAJ SAATHI”; or use the logo to imply a partner is owned by Saj Saathi.
